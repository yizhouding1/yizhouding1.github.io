Manuscript under reviewNumerical methods · Derivative pricing

A Variable-Step Crank–Nicolson and High-Order Compact Scheme for a Nonlinear Black–Scholes Type Equation

Yizhou Ding, Xingyu Zhou, and Pin Lyu

Supervisor: Prof. Pin Lyu, School of Mathematics, SWUFE

Pricing derivatives with counterparty credit risk and funding costs leads to a nonlinear Black–Scholes equation. With my coauthors, I designed a linearized scheme, variable-step Crank–Nicolson in time and fourth-order compact in space, that needs one linear solve per time step without nonlinear iteration. We proved unconditional stability and O(τ² + h⁴) convergence by the energy method, including under negative interest rates, and verified the rates in MATLAB experiments on European options. I led the preparation of the manuscript.

Current research

In progressGIFTS, NTUMarket microstructure · LLM agents

AI Agents in Financial Markets: Simulation and Equilibrium Validation

Research Assistant to Prof. Lin William Cong

I designed an equilibrium agent-based model in which one call-auction market and one damped fixed-point solver are shared by swappable decision engines: exact best response, learning, hybrid LLM, and fully LLM. Exact best responders recover the Kyle equilibria, so deviations can be traced to the decision engine rather than to the market design.

I built its validated base: a multi-group Kyle (1989) equilibrium for 19 investor compositions under two conducts, strategic and price-taking (homotopy, damped Newton), and an ABM of CARA traders learning by recursive least squares that recovers it in 38 of 38 cells (maximum forgone certainty equivalent 0.08%). Fully-LLM markets have been solved to a statistical fixed point in 16 economies under both conducts (13K LLM calls; 30 of 32 cells converge). Preliminary results: LLM traders told to be strategic still largely ignore their price impact, trading near the competitive rule in 12 of 14 economies.

Financial AI Benchmarks: Economic Decisions and On-Chain Execution

Research Assistant to Prof. Lin William Cong

We are designing a benchmark that tests whether LLM agents choose economically sound on-chain actions, not merely execute given ones. It covers four DeFi decision domains (asset allocation, lending and risk management, liquidity provision, and protocol selection) at four difficulty levels, and it scores agents by counterfactual execution on mainnet forks rather than by LLM judges. I built the lending-domain foundation for Aave V3, Compound III, and Morpho Blue from contract code.

Pilot studyGIFTS, NTU · Great Bay UniversityPrediction markets · Market design

AI for Prediction-Market Design and Dispute Prevention

With Prof. Mingzhe Li, Great Bay University

In a pilot study, I rebuilt Polymarket's on-chain history (2.38M markets, oracle disputes, and clarifications) from a Polygon archive node. From launch-time text alone, Mantel–Haenszel odds ratios over 407K markets show that contingency clauses cut dispute odds by about 70%, while “announced/confirmed” triggers raise upheld disputes 2.3×. I also built a matched, time-split benchmark for LLM pre-launch screening.

In progressNanyang Business School, NTUAI · Analyst careers

How AI Reshapes Sell-Side Analysts' Careers

Research Assistant to Prof. Samia Badidi

I mapped 63 target analyst titles onto Revelio's 17K-category role taxonomy, keeping 777 categories, each cross-checked against O*NET. I then built the WRDS employment panel in SQL and Python, filtering 397M raw records by role, industry (NAICS 52, RICS), seniority, intern, and outlier rules into 12.7M positions of 8.2M people at 316K finance firms (2010–2026). An audit of role labels against raw titles, run through live WRDS queries, found a 16% mismatch for the 17K-category label versus 81% for the 1.5K-category one.

Applied research

Galaxy FuturesJan.–May 2025Options · High-frequency data

Short-horizon volatility returns versus option time decay

I tested whether intraday gamma gains of long straddles offset theta decay, using minute-level data for 10+ Chinese commodity options and a liquidity-based contract-selection rule. Mapping intraday volatility seasonality in about 20 commodity futures showed that the straddle paid off for night-session crude oil, but not for PTA. The study was published as a two-part article on Galaxy Futures' WeChat account.
